# Myfoodie BARF Jungle cat food

Unofficial concept ads for Myfoodie (麦富迪) BARF Jungle adult cat food (霸弗丛林食谱天然成猫粮, 1.8 kg), written for Douyin in Chinese at 9:16. A coding agent made both with MixReel, starting from an empty project folder.

Both films sell the same pack and star the same cat. The first puts an offer card on screen at 5.6 seconds. The second tucks the product inside a short story and never asks for a click.

**Selling point.** The pack front prints 「≥80% 肉/骨/内脏」, "at least 80% meat/bone/organs". We read it off the official pack photo on the brand's product page. That photo is only 800×800, so we enlarged it to read the small type, then set the caption in the same order and punctuation as the pack. The film always names all three parts and never shortens the claim to "80% meat".

**Idea.** You pour dinner, and your tabby treats the bowl as prey. It crouches with its rear wiggling and pounces. While it crunches, a deadpan nature-documentary narrator explains what hunters eat and what this pack says. The feeling we aimed for is the owner's pride in a ridiculous cat, plus knowing what is in the bowl.

## How both were made

The agent first built a brand file from official public sources such as the product page and the brand's press releases, each with a link and a date. It also wrote down what the ads may not claim under China's pet feed labeling rules and Advertising Law. That ruled out any health claim or superlative. It also ruled out anything posing as a real customer review. Then it wrote one script per film and blocked every shot in Blender with proxy shapes. The previs changed four camera positions in the performance ad and six in the creator ad, mostly where the cat's body hid the bowl or the owner's face.

One cat reference serves both films. GPT Image generated it once from text alone, a sheet of one adult mackerel tabby in three poses. Every request with the cat carried that sheet. For the owner, Midjourney made the face, and GPT Image completed her body and pajamas without redrawing the face. Each first frame she appears in was edited from that finished portrait.

The work ran in two rounds. A first test round produced complete cuts and showed where the cat clipped through props. The second round regenerated every shot with MiniMax H3 Max Turbo at 1080p, one request per shot, each from a 9:16 first frame. Two creator-ad shots also had a last frame. That round is what ships here. The voices came from MiniMax too. The owner's line and her laugh are the shots' own sound. The cat's inner voice came from separate voice-only requests, with only the audio kept. The Turbo model would not speak an off-screen narrator, so the documentary narration came from a voice-only MiniMax H3 request instead.

QA started with an automated check of the file's format and loudness, which also lists every cut. Then the agent stepped through the first-round cut and every new clip at 5 frames per second, which is where the clipping showed up.

| Film | Problem found at 5 fps | Fix in the shipped cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Performance | Front paws land inside the kibble pile | Pounce regenerated so the paws land on the floor either side of the bowl |
| Performance | A raised paw passes through the bowl rim | Last shot regenerated, keeping a take where the paws stay on the floor |
| Performance | Pack and caption linger one or two frames into the next shot | Overlays composited frame by frame and ended on the cut |
| Performance | Pack looks like it floats behind the bowl | Pack moved and masked so the bowl rim overlaps its base, with a contact shadow |
| Creator | The cat's whole body passes through the box wall | Regenerated so only its head goes in from the far side |
| Creator | The pack's dark side bleeds over the cat's head | The cat stays behind the pack, so no cutout is pasted back |
| Creator | Two frames without the card slip out after it ends | Card extended to the cut |

Last came a listening pass with Gemini, run blind. We learned this the hard way. When the prompt listed the expected lines, Gemini wrote a matching transcript and a high score whatever the audio held, which is how two early 9.2 ratings for the performance ad appeared. After that, Gemini got the audio with no script, transcribed it, and we compared its transcript with ours and checked the loudness envelope. Its scores still wandered, from 6.8 to 9.5 for the same narration, so we used them only as hints.

## Why one product gets two films

The performance ad is for someone scrolling past who might buy today. The offer card with its price button arrives at 5.6 s and returns on the end card. In between it says one number from the pack. The creator ad is for people who follow pet-skit accounts for the story. The pack first appears at 23.8 s, after the cat and the box have held them for a while. Only the cat reads the pack, and there is no price. Take the pack out and the turn of the story collapses, because the cat has nothing left to inspect.

## Cost and known flaws

Generation for the round that ships cost **$6.43** for the performance ad and **$8.96** for the creator ad. Image costs are estimates from token usage at list price. The first test round cost another $4.23 and $6.25, and none of it is in these cuts.

Flaws we left in, from the agent's own review.

Performance ad
- The pounce is a low dash and a short hop, so all four paws leave the floor only briefly.
- The last shot is wide and the cat looks small in it.
- The pack is visible in the reveal shot but missing from the pounce right after. That camera pans, and we did not track the composite.
- The script-synthesized music sounds synthetic next to a real recording.
- The ≥80% text was read from an 800-pixel photo. The brand's own detail page writes it once as 80%肉骨内脏 without the ≥, and we followed the pack.

Creator ad
- The pack in the inspection shot is enlarged 1.18 times to about 61% of the screen width, and it looks slightly soft.
- The cat's voice comes from two generations. Gemini judged them the same speaker, though on a close listen the tone shifts a little.
- The first frame of the wake-up shot is a 1.42 times enlargement of the opening shot's last frame, a little softer than the rest.
- At 52.8 s it runs 10.8 s past the scripted 42 s, mostly because the voices needed more room at a natural pace.
- The Pixabay page showed no Content ID mark, but the platform's matching could still claim the track.
