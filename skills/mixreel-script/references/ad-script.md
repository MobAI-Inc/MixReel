# Advertising message, claims, and review

Product facts come from the advertiser, never from this skill or from a reference video. They live in `brand/brand-context.md`. If that file is missing or still the starter text, [build it first](../../mixreel/references/project.md#build-the-brand-files), and show it to the user before you rely on it.

## This film

Record the audience situation, the one message, the desired action, the length, and any required copy. For each claim, record its source and its conditions. Exact price, a guarantee, "free", and eligibility must match supplied facts. A caption cannot drop the condition that makes a claim true.

Everyday language about the viewer's life is not a product claim. If a viewer could read it as a promise, mark the question instead of deleting it silently.

A concept UI is not a product recording. Label it.

## Product, copy, and UI on screen

Keep review notes out of audience-facing copy. Keep a disclosure when the portrayal would otherwise mislead.

Follow [brand fidelity](../../mixreel/references/brand-fidelity.md). Mark each precise element as a supplied recording, an edit overlay, or an explicitly authorized concept. A screen recording is an existing asset. It does not go through image generation. Do not redraw the product's own layout to fit a camera move.

When the product's screen interrupts a scene, as when the viewer taps a choice or types something, the scene before and after the screen is one scene, with the same place, camera plan, wardrobe and light, in one generation request. When that runs past one request's maximum, split where the screen covers the picture and continue from the previous ending. What the viewer chose or typed is acted out inside the scene. Reserve the screen's time inside the generated timeline so sound runs under it, and take its background from a frame of the same clip.

If the ending asks for an action, the action has to be one the product actually offers.

## Review before delivery

Read the sequence once as a viewer who does not know the product, and once against the brief. For the first reading, hand only the shot table, with no theme, notes or back story, to a fresh agent and ask it to retell what it saw and felt, and whether the payoff needed it to know how much time had passed. A retelling that misses the theme is a failure to fix before delivery.

- Is the message visible in the first seconds?
- Can the film be told as one event, and does it deliver its stated theme read in order?
- Is each cause followed by the face it lands on, with one beat per shot?
- Does any beat depend on a fact the viewer was never shown?
- Are brand, UI, and copy accurate and sourced?
- Did a borrowed dimension leak into appearance or wording?
- Does the film use an approach the user has rejected in the brand record, or give a product or story an idea it has already used, as `brand/films.md` shows?

Do not certify a script as viral or high-converting.
