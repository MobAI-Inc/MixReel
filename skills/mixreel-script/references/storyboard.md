# Shot list

Script step 5, shots, and step 7, the feasibility check. The step list is [the script skill](../SKILL.md).

## Review format

Fill every column of the shot table in [the template](../assets/SCRIPT.template.md). Write `none` where a shot has no copy or no spoken line, so a blank never means undecided. Show the visible sequence with target timing or provisional pacing, picture, action and camera, exact copy and spoken lines, sound and music, and the product and brand treatment. Include the selected reference functions when a reference was used.

Keep a story outline apart from a complete director-facing script. Every actual camera cut in the script gets a stable shot id, a range on the final timeline, scale and viewpoint, subject placement and starting state, one principal camera behavior, visible action and reaction, the ending state, and how it joins the next shot. A beat containing several setups needs several shot rows. Each shot should say what it reveals or changes, in information or in feeling. A timestamp alone is not a storyboard. Do not deliver a timed paragraph in place of shots.

Mark how each shot will be made. Video is generated by a video model. Image is one illustration held or moved in the edit, such as a comic-style panel. Post is made in the edit layer, such as product UI, a supplied screen recording, captions, or an end card. Continuous action, a moving camera, or people interacting need video. A film told in illustrated panels may need none.

When a previs is used, also write positions and facing so the scene file can be built. A locked-off pack shot or a supplied screen recording does not need a walking path.

A script-only task needs no paid account or generated assets. Before finalizing a full script, run the early feasibility check below. [Video planning](../../mixreel-video-plan/SKILL.md) owns the final groups, verified request settings, and the frames each group needs. Keep shot ids across that handoff. Camera cuts do not imply separate calls. Provider prompts are a later stage. Leave them empty here.

## Beats and reactions

Write each stretch's shots from its beats. List in order every beat the viewer has to see, and give each its own shot. A beat is what one person does or feels in one continuous movement, such as picking up the phone and glancing at it, and it stays in one shot. What happens on the other person's side is a reverse shot, and a focus pull does not replace that cut. A run of separate actions, such as climbing the stairs, setting down the box, and finding the door already open, is several beats.

Every cause the viewer has to register is followed by the face it lands on, because that reaction is how she learns what the cause meant, such as the stain lifting and then her looking up. A look is a cause too. A face hidden for a later reveal still owes its reaction, so when that reaction carries the beat, such as his disappointment that the wrong person came in, cut in close to show it. A feeling that changes does so in steps, each a cause shot and a reaction shot. A morning going wrong reads as the alarm and her smile at the sun, the empty coffee tin and her puzzled face, the bus pulling away and her flat stare, rain on her white shirt and her shut eyes, then her point of view as the delivery app shows the coffee two minutes away.

When one person's action lands on another, such as a handoff, a spill, or a surprise, give it at least three shots, the contact, the one it lands on, and the one who did it, so the viewer sees what the action did to both. The simplest handoff runs like this. Her hand in close-up presses an ice-cold can into his palm, and his hand flinches at the chill. His face, close, surprised, turning toward her off frame. Her in a medium shot, grinning, already turning back to the grill. Him in a medium shot, cracking the can open and calling after her. More force or more at stake earns more shots between the contact and the last reaction, never fewer than these three.

Cut as soon as each change reads and let the next shot carry its result. The can meets his palm and the film is already on his reaction. A shot inside a chain does not settle into an ending of its own, which slows the chain and tells the viewer nothing new. The hold belongs at the end of the chain, on the last reaction or the final image.

A shot asks for the time its change needs to read. A door opening and the focus moving to whoever comes in takes about three seconds, while a beat inside a fast action can be under one. When the film has time to fill, it comes from finer shots, never from squeezing several changes into one or holding a shot past its change. A continuous action and its finer shots stay in one generation request. Music offers cut points all through it, so take the one nearest to where the action changes, and never bend the action to a beat.

Carry the change on faces, hands, and large actions. Detail a video model cannot render legibly, such as small text on a phone, belongs in a post shot with time to read it, or out of the film.

## Editable scene description

Serialize each scene description as `{"description":"..."}` when a tool needs structured input. The prose holds the following, in natural order.

- Establish the view and the subjects' physical relationship, including what is outside the frame.
- Give the camera one principal behavior and a destination. For a fixed camera, explicitly rule out dolly, zoom, and pan. This wording applies only when the shot is meant to be fixed.
- Describe observable actions and reactions in their causal order. For a risky action, make the starting state, the contact or force, the transition, and the resulting state visible. When the usual way of performing it would break a relationship, write the specific safe pose.
- State where the shot ends, which inside a chain that cuts on the change can be mid-motion, and what must stay readable there. Give the product and props that matter their own sentences. In a shot where a prop must stay out of frame, describe where the eyes go instead of naming it.
- Specify synchronous environment and action sound when relevant. Record score and edit-layer audio apart from scene sound, and keep the treatment the user chose.

Keep this description free of model settings, duration ceilings, reference lists, URLs, style headers, readable overlays, and fade-to-black instructions. Do not repeat wardrobe the visual references already supply. Put technical timing and appearance bindings in the fields beside it. This exclusion applies only to the generation-facing scene field. Put approved captions, titles, UI, brand marks, and ending treatments in the script and edit-layer fields so production can see them.

The editable scene description is a review artifact. MiniMax H3 may take several approved shots and reads the approved scene and shot plan directly. It must not inherit a one-setup Seedance prompt as its source. Read the selected [Seedance 2](../../mixreel-video-plan/references/seedance-2.md) or [MiniMax H3](../../mixreel-video-plan/references/minimax-h3.md) notes only after the model is chosen.

The provider prompt is a separate rendering of the approved generation group, combining its ordered shot descriptions and continuity requirements. It can add ordered reference bindings, supported timing hints, and locked style instructions. It must keep the action, gaze, cause and effect, and the authored ending. Never let a provider adapter rewrite the film to fit a familiar prompt template.

## Continuity and variation

Track positions, screen direction, eye lines, who holds which prop, the product's state, wardrobe, environment, and time of day across cuts. Keep each person's user-selected outfit the same within a continuous scene, using the wardrobe-reference rules in [images](../../mixreel-keyframes/references/images.md). Write the visual cause of each meaningful change. A change of angle is fine when geography stays understandable. There is no automatic ban on repeated angles or static shots.

Ask what each shot adds, in information or in feeling, and choose its scale and angle for that, such as a wide view for scale, an over-shoulder view for being watched or shut out, an insert for evidence, or a close-up for a reaction. The scene justifies these choices. They are not a checklist that forces every type into every film.

Choose camera movement or stillness for what the viewer needs to discover and how the action develops. Do not lock every shot because it is easier to describe, and do not add motion only to make the list look varied. Record the purpose of a camera choice that matters in the production notes.

Check that the starting state, action, and ending can follow one another within the slot. Adjust staging or timing before generation when they cannot.

A montage can connect moments that are not consecutive through a deliberate emotional progression. Label this structure so the missing cause and effect is clearly intended. A continuous scene requires continuous geography and action.

## Approval scope

Present the actual visual plan before illustration. Record the artifact revision and the user's response. Changing the idea, the feeling it rests on, or major staging invalidates the reviews that depend on it. Correcting a typo does not. If the user asks only for a proposal, stop after delivering it. Generation stays a later task.

## Dialogue

Before writing a line, know how each speaker talks and what they want right now. An ad is not a short drama. Keep its lines few and quick to catch on first hearing, and let the picture carry the rest. Inside that limit they still have to sound like people, because a film of clipped poses sounds cold and everyone in it turns into the same person.

- **The want leaks out.** The line shows what the speaker wants or fears, even while hiding it. A boast with no want behind it is a pose.
- **People talk from their own world.** A mechanic talks about what cannot be fixed, a cook about what has gone cold.
- **Questions and bets hand the moment over.** Orders end it.
- **A noticed detail beats a compliment.** It shows that this person was paying attention.
- **Exchanges stay even.** The other person can answer back, and a line gets answered.
- **Rhythm varies.** One slightly longer honest line among short ones hits hardest. In an ad even that stays around a dozen words.
- **A stranger still has to follow it.** When the picture cannot show what is at stake, a line carries it, in words someone in that room would use. A line meant to move the viewer is said to the person she stands in for, about her, not about the speaker's own story. Nobody recites full names, the setting, or a feature list. Vows, declarations, and lines that explain the moment sound childish.
- **Time it aloud.** A line that will not fit its shot changes the shot or goes. It does not speed up.

Test each line. Could another person in the film say it unchanged? What does the speaker want right now? Would the viewer lose a beat of feeling without it?

## Early feasibility check

Use known user constraints and supplied capability evidence while writing, instead of discovering after approval that the script cannot be produced. Record the total edit duration, which shots are made as video, image, and post, which are a real recording, and any provisional generation groups. Note any limit you have already verified from the provider's own docs. Unknown limits stay unknown. Do not quote a price you have not been given. A product screen inside a scene follows [product, copy, and UI on screen](ad-script.md#product-copy-and-ui-on-screen), including the time it takes. If a stated request maximum is exceeded, propose a continuation from the previous request's ending as described in [video planning](../../mixreel-video-plan/references/planning.md), coherent group boundaries, or a shorter treatment. Label limits the user gave you apart from endpoint support you verified yourself. Never turn one project's limit into a universal model rule.

For each provisional boundary, name the positions of people and props that carry over, the ending and restarting state, whether time continues or is deliberately skipped, and the visual and audio bridge. Keep enough time for actions and readable text. Split at a pause, a reaction, or an edit-layer insert when one fits, instead of in the middle of a critical movement. A generated clip's minimum billable duration does not decide the speed of that movement.

A full script also needs a sound decision. Follow the user's chosen track or sound direction. Without one, propose reusing or adapting reference audio you are allowed to use, a newly composed score, or a deliberate sound-only treatment if it fits the request. Unknown alignment can stay provisional, but "music pending" alone is not a finished sound plan. Specify cue intervals, mood and instruments or sound texture, accents, room for spoken lines, and the ending. Keep observed source audio apart from invented direction. Do not claim to imitate audio you have not heard.

When you propose music composed by the video model, carry that requirement into video planning and the selected prompt's score field. Verify native audio and music output before execution. Prompt text cannot give a silent endpoint that capability. Separate requests do not guarantee a shared tune, key, or seamless score. Plan a supported shared score or an editing method and the assets it needs. Keep the music running through the film. Duck it under decisive moments instead of stopping and restarting it, and split between two pieces only for a deliberate before-and-after contrast. Unresolved capability blocks submit-readiness. It does not block review of the creative proposal. This check authorizes no new model call.

Decide where the final score comes from before any generation. If an edit-layer track will carry the music, ask the video model for spoken lines, synchronous sound, and ambience without score, so that sound can be kept when the track is laid in. A score mixed into generated audio cannot be removed cleanly later. If the model composes the score, give its tempo, cue points, and accents, place planned cuts and hits on that grid, and keep that score through the whole film. When the user later swaps the music, rebuild the cut on the new beat grid and report which generated sounds can no longer be reused.

## Reference sheet

When the project has real images for these shots, lay them out in one `reference-sheet.jpg` in the film folder, with Pillow or ffmpeg. Use frames from `brand/taste-library/` and files from `brand/assets/`, each labelled with its shot id and source file. Nothing on the sheet is generated, so this does not break the rule about showing the script before any illustration. When the project has no such images, skip the sheet.

Every scene told in more than one shot in one place gets a [previs](previs.md).
