# Cinematic coverage

Open this only while writing a MiniMax H3 prompt, before the [H3 format](h3-format.md). Otherwise close it and return to [the video-plan skill](../SKILL.md).

This file writes prompts only. It does not submit generation jobs. Use it to turn the approved visible event into a short shot sequence before you write H3 syntax.

## Keep the script before directing

The approved scene controls the event, action order, positions, gaze, product and props, reactions, and final image. Coverage may show those facts more clearly. It may not replace them. If the script says `close-up`, `cut to`, `from inside`, `through the window`, `tracking`, or another concrete camera decision, keep it unless it creates a direct physical contradiction.

Do not collapse two places into one convenient composition. A person seen through a window stays on the stated side of the window. A reaction that comes after an action stays after it. A final product shot or gesture still owns the final frame.

## Decide when to cut

Cut when one composition cannot clearly carry the information. Common reasons:

- A wide action and a small facial reaction both matter.
- The viewer must understand what one person sees and then read that person's response.
- The product or a prop changes state, and the change would be unclear in the master shot.
- The scene moves between distinct spaces or sides of a threshold.
- A held-back touch, a kept distance, or an eyeline carries the beat.

Keep a continuous shot when unbroken time, physical distance, mounting discomfort, or one flowing action is itself the point. A single shot is a deliberate choice, not a default.

## Direct the atmosphere

Find the scene's emotional arc, not only its main feeling. Mark the opening state, the beat that changes or intensifies it, and what is left in the final image. If the script has no turn, keep a deliberately still mood. State the atmosphere once in the description, then let things the camera and microphone can catch carry it: shot distance, pace, stillness, gaze, breath, a small hesitation, light, weather, movement in the setting, physical sound, touch, and a touch held back.

Let atmosphere shape coverage without hiding the event. A change in the product's state stays readable, and a beat between two people still shows who chooses, who waits, and who reaches out.

Do a silent-frame check before you finish the plan. The emotional change should still read from performance, distance, light, or composition with the dialogue and sound removed. Sound can deepen the scene but should not carry the whole turn by itself.

## Make decisive actions complete

The shots around an action that lands on another person come from the approved script, as [storyboard](../../mixreel-script/references/storyboard.md#beats-and-reactions) describes. This section writes the action inside its shot. For an action that matters, show a short chain of cause instead of naming the result. Establish the starting state, make the contact or force visible, show the transition, then make the result readable, in this shot or in the next one that carries it. The model should see where the hand, tool, object, and resistance are relative to one another.

Do not hide the decisive contact behind a body, cut away before the state changes, or ask one brief insert to perform several unrelated actions. Prefer positive placement such as `both shoes stay one step behind the threshold` over a bare negative such as `do not enter`.

## Two-person coverage

Begin with a master or a two-shot when the viewer needs the geography. Move closer when a gaze, a hesitation, a refusal, or a reaction changes the meaning of the exchange.

For a spoken or silent exchange, choose from these tools for the scene:

- A two-shot establishes distance, obstruction, or who holds an object.
- An over-the-shoulder shot connects one person's look to the other person's response.
- A reverse shot gives the second person an equal point of view.
- A clean close-up isolates a reaction the other person does not notice.
- A shared final frame shows the relationship after the action has changed it.

Keep to the 180-degree line unless the script deliberately disorients the viewer. Match eyelines, screen direction, hand positions, and who holds what through every reverse angle.

When a boundary, a kept distance, or who holds the product is the beat, design a frame that proves it. Keep the hands, feet, gap, threshold, obstruction, or object in the same readable frame. Do not let the final angle flatten two people onto the same plane when their separation is the point.

## Environment shots and inserts

An environment shot may establish place, weather, isolation, or the direction of travel. It can also bridge two spaces. Keep it brief enough that the event still fits the duration.

A product or prop insert may reveal evidence, mark a change of state, or make a transition. Come back from the insert with positions and motion continuous.

Do not add an empty shot or insert only to imitate a trailer. Every added image carries information, mood tied to the setting, or a transition the script already supports.

## Camera movement

Choose camera behavior per shot. One shot may track while a later reaction shot stays locked. Within one shot, use one principal move so the model can keep composition and action.

Describe movement concretely, such as `tracks sideways beside the cyclist`, `slowly pushes from a two-shot into the listener's close-up`, or `stays locked on the doorway`. Avoid stacked moves that compete for the same short interval.

## Timing

Choose the group's duration after the shot plan is complete. A cut inside the group does not need another generated file, and a fast action can finish well before its shot or file ends. There is no default clip length and no fixed shot count. Let the visible event decide.

Shot lengths come from the approved script, timed as [storyboard](../../mixreel-script/references/storyboard.md#beats-and-reactions) describes. A spoken line still needs its natural time. Add them and round up to a whole second. Use the verified minimum duration when the total is shorter. When the total exceeds the verified maximum, remove decorative coverage or merge only shots that stay readable, and report that the event needs several clips if its beats still do not fit. Choose only durations the endpoint supports. Having used a duration on another scene is not a reason to choose it.

Give each shot enough time for its action to read. Do not place a cut during the decisive change unless the cut itself carries it, such as shears closing in close-up followed by the tyre landing in a wider shot.
